## Context

<!-- segment: S0030 -->
The priesthood was incredibly corrupt before and during Jesus’s lifetime. Bribes determined the next high priest instead of lots drawn by members of the Levite tribe. Wealthy Sadducees paid to be appointed in exchange for keeping peace with Rome and enforcing Roman law; as compensation, they benefited from temple tithes and animal sales. Understanding this corruption helps us understand Jesus’s actions in the temple—His Father’s house. God required spotless sacrifices. But the priesthood, the Annas family, inspected all sacrifices and frequently denied them, forcing people to purchase available animals right there in the temple, specifically in the Court of the Gentiles, at insanely inflated prices. The Annases profited off of the Jews who made the pilgrimage to Jerusalem for Passover, the annual festival celebrating the exodus from Egypt. Not only did they inflate prices, but they also charged temple taxes. The market-like atmosphere hindered Gentiles from worshiping. This context set the stage for Jesus’s righteous indignation at what the sacrificial system of atonement had become—a system He came to replace with His own body, the Lamb of God.

### Theology Connection

<!-- segment: S0032 -->
#### God Is Holy

<!-- segment: S0033 -->
God’s holiness refers to His uniqueness in being separate from all He has created. The Hebrew word for “holy” means “separate” or “set apart.” God’s holiness also refers to His absolute purity. God is unstained by the evil of the world. His goodness is perfect, and the moral code we find in the Scriptures is a reflection of His holy nature. As people made in God’s image, we are called to holiness.

### 2. CULTURAL CONNECTION

<!-- segment: S0047 -->
What is worship? How do we worship God the right way? We use the word “worship” a lot in Christian culture, but the word even bleeds into secular, or worldly, culture. We say things like, “She worships the ground he walks on” and make people, brands, and activities our idols. Only God is worthy of worship, but we often bow before lesser “gods”—false gods that can’t save us, gods that lead us away from the one true God and to destruction. Worship is giving God the glory and praise He deserves; worship involves our words, but even more, worship is bowing our hearts before the Lord and living in submission to Him. Here’s how we know we’re worshiping God the right way: We examine what our love and adoration point to; we examine what we’re devoted to; we ask if our heart is divided between God and anything else. And if it is, we lay that thing down in surrender. Worship requires us to surrender our hearts, minds, and actions to the only One who is worthy.

**WHEN SOMEONE DISRESPECTS YOU OR SOMEONE YOU LOVE, HOW DO YOU TYPICALLY REACT?**

<!-- segment: S0054 -->
A core human desire is the need to feel valued. Most of us have been disrespected at some point, causing us to feel small and invisible. Maybe someone said something harmful about our character or insulted our family. Some of us handle being disrespected better than watching family members, people we love, or people who are vulnerable be mistreated. We rise up to defend them and may feel immediate anger, a desire for revenge, or the need to fix the situation. **In the moment, we just want to restore value to ourselves and the people we care about.**

<!-- segment: S0055 -->
**WHAT DOES JESUS’S REACTION HELP YOU UNDERSTAND ABOUT ANGER?**

<!-- segment: S0056 -->
Many of us misunderstand anger, believing it’s a sin. While anger can lead to sin, it’s an emotion—one God feels perfectly and righteously. God is slow to anger (Ps. 103:8). Paul instructed believers to “be angry and do not sin” (Eph. 4:26). **This means that we can be angry without sinning. How? Selfish anger and righteous anger have different motives.** Righteous anger stirs us to action and motivates us to stand against sin and injustice. Jesus displayed righteous anger. His anger wasn’t rooted in revenge or pride, like ours so often is, but in respect for God’s holiness, with the goal of restoring acceptable worship in the temple.

<!-- segment: S0057 -->
**IN WHAT WAYS DO WE _CHEAPEN_ WORSHIP OR TREAT GOD’S HOUSE WITH DISRESPECT?**

<!-- segment: S0058 -->
_Leaders: Share context that set the stage for this event._ The sacrificial system in the Old Testament and in Jesus’s time was broken. What God intended as provision for humans to experience forgiveness of sin transitioned into a money-making scheme that took advantage of people. What was supposed to be worshipful morphed into a corrupted show, distracting people from worship. God demanded a better sacrifice than what the people were bringing—even with inflated prices. If we aren’t careful, we can be guilty of cheapening worship too. How? By appearing to worship God but being far from Him in our hearts. By looking the part at church but living like the world. By singing worship songs under bright lights and fog but not living the way God calls us to live in His Word. By making worship and church about _us_ instead of God. By not offering Him our best. Here’s the truth: Only pure sacrifice makes us right with God and enables us to worship Him. **God’s holiness demands a better sacrifice than we could ever bring. Our better sacrifice is _Jesus_. He is the only acceptable sacrifice. He covered our sin fully, permanently. When we make worship about us rather than God, we cheapen His sacrifice.**

<!-- segment: S0059 -->
### JESUS IS THE BETTER TEMPLE

<!-- segment: S0060 -->
**READ JOHN 2:18-25**

<!-- segment: S0061 -->
**WHY DID THE JEWS ASK FOR A SIGN (V. 18)? IS THERE EVER AN OKAY REASON TO ASK GOD FOR A SIGN?**

<!-- segment: S0062 -->
After Jesus cleared the temple courts, the Jews asked for a sign. In other words, they asked for proof of Jesus’s authority to make a claim that the temple was His Father’s house. Why? When Jesus said this, He claimed to be God’s Son, and this was a big statement that could warrant investigation from Jewish leaders. **They didn’t want a sign so that they could believe. They asked for a sign out of a desire to prove Jesus was a fraud.** Other times in the Bible, God did sometimes honor the request for a sign (Ex. Gideon, Abraham’s servant). The difference was that God provided these signs to strengthen people’s faith and to empower them to accomplish His plan. **We can ask God to confirm His will for us while consulting His Word first, seeking Him in prayer, and seeking wise advice from godly mentors.**

<!-- segment: S0063 -->
**WHAT DID JESUS REALIZE ABOUT MANY OF THE PEOPLE WHO BELIEVED IN HIM AFTER THE SIGNS? WHAT DOES THIS TYPE OF BELIEF LOOK LIKE TODAY?**

<!-- segment: S0064 -->
Because Jesus is omniscient, or all-knowing, He saw through the people’s exterior and knew their hearts. Their “belief” was dependent on signs Jesus performed and not in the truth that He was the Messiah, the Son of God. Like them, we often chase God’s works over knowing Him. We believe only if God does what we ask of Him. Our belief isn’t rooted in faith—in absolute assurance that Jesus is Lord. **If attached to signs, our belief unravels when life hurts, when healing doesn’t come, when we feel like God doesn’t answer our prayers. But belief rooted in faith, in the assurance of who Jesus is and in the hope of the gospel, is unwavering.** Signs are unnecessary because Jesus’s life, death, and resurrection are enough.

<!-- segment: S0065 -->
**WHY DOES JESUS COMPARE HIMSELF TO THE TEMPLE?**

<!-- segment: S0066 -->
The temple was crucial to the Jews’ identity. The temple provided a center for sacrifice and a way for God to dwell among His people. The temple had been destroyed and rebuilt, so Jesus’s statement sounded like a radical threat. But Jesus spoke of *Himself* as the temple. Jesus is God, and He dwelled among His people as the center for sacrifice—as *the* sacrifice. **He surrendered His body, allowing it to fall, but after three days, He raised back to life! The temple was always a shadow of Jesus, the better temple.**

## Wrap-Up

<!-- segment: S0068 -->
**WHEN HAVE YOU FELT LIKE YOU DIDN’T HAVE ANYTHING GOOD TO BRING TO GOD? HOW CAN YOU REST IN JESUS’S PERFECT SACRIFICE?**

<!-- segment: S0069 -->
**God is holy. He can’t accept half-hearted, insincere worship. Our cheap sacrifices can never be worthy enough for us to enter God’s holy presence; our best sacrifices rooted in our own goodness can never be enough either. God requires complete surrender, which can feel a little intimidating, right? Here’s the other part: God requires our best but gave His Son to be our best offering. He demands everything, but Jesus lowered Himself to satisfy the demand and was everything on our behalf (Phil. 2:7-8). Jesus’s perfect sacrifice covers us and atones for our sin. He purchased our freedom and our rest. So, we don’t have to strive to be worthy because the blood of Jesus makes us worthy! God still deserves our best, but instead of striving to earn salvation, our best is rooted in gratitude for what Jesus has already done. His work on the cross was enough. And now, we have the privilege of living our lives as offerings of praise.**
