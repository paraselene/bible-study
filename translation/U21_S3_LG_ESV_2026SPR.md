## Context

<!-- segment: S0032 -->
Who was **Nicodemus**, the man who conversed with Jesus at night? First, Nicodemus was one of the Pharisees, a group of roughly 6,000 men deeply committed to obeying and enforcing God’s law—613 commands, along with additional laws the Jews added to ensure their obedience. He likely was a member of the Sanhedrin as well—seventy men who acted as the governing body of the Jewish nation. In other words, Nicodemus had all the right credentials. He was a highly-respected, knowledgeable teacher of the law. In the Jews’ eyes, Nicodemus was the epitome of one fit for God’s kingdom, but the right credentials, even religious ones, can’t ensure a place there. This concept would’ve been foreign to Nicodemus, who believed his lineage and self-righteousness made him secure. The radical idea that neither of these mattered led to more questions and, ultimately, Jesus unraveling the mystery of the gospel for Nicodemus and for us: No one can enter or see the kingdom without looking to the Son in faith. Our best credentials are polluted by sin, so God gave His Son to keep us from perishing (John 3:16).

<!-- segment: S0033 -->
### Theology Connection

<!-- segment: S0034 -->
#### Regeneration

<!-- segment: S0035 -->
Regeneration takes place at the beginning of the Christian life and is the miraculous transformation, or the new birth, that takes place within an individual through the supernatural work of the Holy Spirit (John 3:3-8; Titus 3:5). It is the divine side of conversion (a person turning to Christ in repentance and faith), being the work of God within a person’s life that causes him or her to be born again, a work that human effort is unable to produce.

### 2. CULTURAL CONNECTION

<!-- segment: S0050 -->
You’ve probably messed up before and wanted to just start over. Maybe you made a horrible grade and wanted to retake a test. Maybe you gossiped about a friend, and you’d love to undo your words and the damage they caused. Maybe you sinned and did something you never thought you’d do, and you’d love to go back in time to make the right choice. In some situations, we can start over, but when it comes to our sin, we can’t. We can’t go back to the beginning and choose never to sin. Why? When Adam and Eve fell to sin, all of humanity was infected by it. We are born into a sinful world, and we can’t fix it. This may sound hopeless, but it just means that we needed someone else to fix it for us—someone never infected with sin. Jesus’s sacrifice provides something better than a second chance because, if given another chance, we’d squander it. Instead, we are reborn! When we call on Jesus for salvation, He gives us His Spirit. We are renewed, continuously covered by Jesus’s blood and no longer bound to our sin!

### JESUS OFFERS US NEW LIFE

<!-- segment: S0055 -->
**READ JOHN 3:1-13**

<!-- segment: S0056 -->
**HAVE YOU EVER WANTED TO ASK GOD A QUESTION? WHAT WOULD YOU ASK?**

<!-- segment: S0057 -->
Maybe you have always wondered why God created spiders, or maybe you’re curious about something more serious, like why a prayer wasn’t answered the way you hoped. Nicodemus had questions too, which led him to meet with Jesus at night. Maybe Nicodemus feared what other leaders would think or maybe he had some other reason for choosing night, but Nicodemus went to the source for answers. Jesus wasn’t offended; He welcomed the conversation. **We naturally wonder about things we struggle to understand, but God isn’t offended when we bring our questions to Him in a search for truth.**

<!-- segment: S0058 -->
**WHAT WAS NICODEMUS’S OPINION OF JESUS? WHY WASN’T IT ENOUGH?**

<!-- segment: S0059 -->
When Nicodemus approached Jesus at night, he called Him “Rabbi”—a term that indicated respect and meant “teacher.” Nicodemus also acknowledged that God was somehow empowering Jesus to perform signs and miracles. **He recognized Jesus was from God, but he didn’t understand that Jesus is God—the promised Messiah.** Nicodemus’s good opinion of Jesus wasn’t enough. How does this apply to us? **Many of us think highly of Jesus; we may even acknowledge that He existed. But good opinions of Him aren’t enough.** We must be born again; we must confess Him as Lord and call on Him for salvation, surrendering wholly to Him.

<!-- segment: S0060 -->
**DOES THE TRUTH THAT YOU CAN’T BE _GOOD ENOUGH_ TO SEE GOD’S KINGDOM COMFORT YOU OR CONCERN YOU? WHY?**

<!-- segment: S0061 -->
_Leaders: Discuss Context about Nicodemus to ensure students understand his position in Jewish culture._ Some of us find comfort in our religious or worldly credentials. We celebrate checking off the right boxes: We go to church; we read our Bibles; we sing in the choir; we make good decisions; we try to be honest; we don’t lie, cheat, or steal; we don’t use profanity or party; we have good morals. All of this sounds really good, doesn’t it? **But if Jesus looked at a Pharisee and member of the Sanhedrin, a man who worked to know and follow God’s law his entire life, and essentially said, “Your goodness isn’t enough; you don’t qualify,” then what does He say of our goodness?** Jesus looked at one of the best of the Jews and told him that he wasn’t fit to see the Father’s kingdom. But this doesn’t have to be bad news! In fact, this is the good news of the gospel. **We _get_ to see and live _now_ as members of the kingdom because Jesus makes us worthy. He imparted His goodness to us, making us righteous with the gift of His blood!** We are reborn—new creations!

<!-- segment: S0062 -->
### JESUS OFFERS US FREEDOM FROM CONDEMNATION

<!-- segment: S0063 -->
**READ JOHN 3:14-21**

<!-- segment: S0064 -->
**WHY DO PEOPLE SOMETIMES DOUBT GOD’S LOVE FOR THEM? HOW DEEP IS GOD’S LOVE FOR US?**

<!-- segment: S0065 -->
Human suffering is one of the biggest reasons people doubt God’s love. We wrestle with why God lets us walk through pain if He loves us. Suffering and grieving dull our understanding. God deeply loves His Son, but He allowed Him to suffer. Why? Because He loves us deeply too (3:16). God doesn’t desire for us to suffer; we can look at Eden and see God’s original design was for us to enjoy peace, perfection, and unbroken relationship with Him. Pain and suffering exist because of sin—because humans allowed the enemy to warp truth. **But in His goodness, God doesn’t waste our pain. He uses it to draw us closer to Him—to remind us that this world is broken and that His only Son was broken and bruised to redeem us.** God is near when we’re hurting (Ps. 34:18). If we reframe suffering as a reminder of our brokenness, then we can stand hopefully under the weight of it, knowing God has promised that all suffering has an expiration date. Instead of allowing the enemy to use our suffering, we can lean into truth.

<!-- segment: S0066 -->
**WHAT COULD YOU SHARE WITH PEOPLE WHO FEEL LIKE GOD JUST WANTS TO CONDEMN THEM?**

<!-- segment: S0067 -->
We feel judged and condemned when someone points out our mistakes. Sometimes, people do have a motive to condemn us, but God’s heart is our freedom. **Jesus explained that He came to redeem us, not condemn us. The truth is that our sin condemns us.** God would’ve been justified in leaving us here without hope, but He came down: Jesus dwelled with us, lived like us, experienced pain, grief, and suffering. Jesus, though sinless, took the condemnation we deserved in exchange for our freedom. God desires our salvation, not our condemnation.

<!-- segment: S0068 -->
**WHAT DOES IT LOOK LIKE FOR TEENAGERS TO LOVE AND CHOOSE DARKNESS TODAY (V. 19)?**

<!-- segment: S0069 -->
Our flesh craves sin; without the transforming power of the Holy Spirit, we choose darkness. Sin masks itself as fun and freedom, when really, sin is slavery. We choose things like partying, alcohol, sexual sin. We avoid light—truth—because then we’d have to make a choice: light or dark, follow God or the world. One is our freedom; one is our bondage. **We feel safer crouching in the dark, but redemption is in the light—in allowing God to expose our sin and our need for Jesus.**

## Wrap-Up

<!-- segment: S0071 -->
**HOW CAN YOU BE AN INSTRUMENT OF LIGHT IN THE WORLD?**

<!-- segment: S0072 -->
**WHAT AREAS OF YOUR LIFE LOOK MORE LIKE DARKNESS THAN LIGHT?**

<!-- segment: S0073 -->
**We’re conditioned to fear the dark. Even as small children, we associate darkness with scary monsters lurking and waiting for us. Jesus drew on this theme of darkness and light because we so easily identify with it. When it comes to our physical safety, we run to the light; when it comes to our sin, we crouch in the dark. We don’t want our sin exposed in the light of day because we’d experience shame, guilt, and more—we’d have to choose either to stay in the dark or to walk into the light. Jesus said believers are light in a dark world (Matt. 5:14). We live as lights when we walk according to God’s Word. This means we’ll live differently from the world. We’ll talk differently; we’ll treat others with love and forgive enemies; we’ll seek holiness instead of popularity; we’ll use language that points to Jesus instead of cutting people down; we’ll share the gospel instead of hiding our faith. If we’ve seen the light, then we must walk in the light. When we do, we lead others to freedom and show them how to leave darkness behind: by trusting the Light of the world (John 8:12).**
