## THEOLOGY CONNECTION

<!-- segment: S0018 -->
### JUSTIFICATION BY FAITH

<!-- segment: S0019 -->
Justification refers to the moment when a person is declared righteous before God based on the righteousness of Jesus’s atoning death (Rom. 8:33-34). This act of declaration takes place through faith in Jesus and not as a result of human works or effort (Eph. 2:8-9). Through justification, a person is made to be in right standing before God, changing what was once a strained and hostile relationship to one of adoption into the family of God.

## CULTURAL CONNECTION

<!-- segment: S0028 -->
Have you ever wrestled with why God heals some people and not others? Christians aren’t immune to the effects of living in a fallen, broken world. Jesus often healed people’s sicknesses and diseases, and Scripture encourages us to pray for healing when we are sick (James 5:14,16). Sometimes God heals, but sometimes He chooses not to heal on this side of eternity. If you’ve walked through a situation where God didn’t answer a prayer for healing, you aren’t alone in feeling disappointed, hurt, or confused. We don’t have to hide our hurt or disappointment from God. Instead, we go to Him; we hold on to the truth that He is our good, loving Father, who uses everything—even what’s painful—to draw us close and make us more like Jesus (Rom. 8:28). We can invite Jesus into our suffering, knowing He sympathizes with our weaknesses and struggles because He also suffered here (Heb. 4:15). God always heals those who seek Him for spiritual healing. All who confess Jesus as Lord are saved (Rom. 10:9), and we have hope that no matter what happens on earth, we will spend eternity in heaven with no more pain, sickness, or disease.

# DAY ONE

<!-- segment: S0030 -->
**EXODUS 14:19-31**

<!-- segment: S0031 -->
Can you imagine how the Israelites felt as they came up on the body of water? The situation must’ve felt desperate: the Egyptians behind them, the water in front of them, and nowhere to go. Can you picture walking on dry land through the walls of the sea, knowing that just a few minutes earlier, there was no way out? They had been slaves in Egypt for 400 years before God sent Moses to rescue them, so we can understand any confusion they had when God powerfully parted the waters. Not long after this miraculous moment, the people started complaining about hunger, accusing God of leading them into the wilderness to die. As outsiders looking in, we get frustrated with the Israelites and are shocked that they want to go back to Egypt—to slavery. But imagine if they were reading a story about your life and how God has worked to rescue you; would they also get frustrated over how quickly you forget and turn to worldly comforts to satisfy your desires or to fit in?

<!-- segment: S0032 -->
Why do we so easily forget? Our world, bodies, and minds are broken. Sin easily distracts us with a promise of temporary pleasure, but God is more wonderful than those fleeting moments. By sending Jesus to take the punishment for your sins, He has done more for you than anything the world can offer you. When you call on the Lord for salvation, you become the dwelling place for the Holy Spirit, who works to transform your heart and mind. The next time you find yourself in a desperate situation, remember that the same power that parted the Red Sea is the same power living inside of you.

# DAY TWO

<!-- segment: S0037 -->
**HEBREWS 11:1-3**

<!-- segment: S0038 -->
Most of us don’t immediately trust people we’ve just met. Trusting new people is much easier when another trusted friend has already tested and approved of their character and integrity. In a way, the Bible does this for us. Scripture is full of people who lived lives just like us—who faced moments requiring great faith in God to have their back. Because of these testimonies in Scripture, when you surrender your life to Christ through faith, you aren’t acting in *blind faith*. God has proven over and over again that He takes care of His people.

<!-- segment: S0039 -->
Hebrews 11 is known as the “Hall of Faith” because the people listed there faced circumstances beyond their control. Many times, they didn’t know what was going to happen, but they took the next step in faith because they trusted the One guiding their steps. They had unwavering faith in God because they witnessed His unwavering commitment to work all things for their good and His glory (Rom. 8:28).

<!-- segment: S0040 -->
God is for us; He is committed to us. We can move confidently through the unknown because we know God’s character through the stories in His Word. You can’t see God with your eyes, but your relationship with Him is as real as it gets, as real as your relationship with your closest friend. If you are waiting for a clear sign from God to know if you can trust Him, you can look in His Word. You can confidently move in faith because God has already proven He is faithful to take care of you.

# DAY THREE

<!-- segment: S0045 -->
**JOHN 4:39-45**

<!-- segment: S0046 -->
Have you ever been to a football game in your hometown? An interesting thing happens as the athletes on the field grow up and sit in the stands as adults. People often still see them as who they were when they were students.

<!-- segment: S0047 -->
Today’s reading is the perfect example of this phenomenon. Jesus met with a Samaritan woman and was flooded with other Samaritans, who were eager to meet Him. The Jews were God’s chosen people from which Jesus descended, and they despised the Samaritans. But Jesus gave them His time and attention anyway. As Jesus left for Galilee, He said that a man does not have honor in his hometown. If Jesus was to sit in the football stands in Galilee, the people would struggle to see Him as anyone other than the boy they watched grow up there. They didn’t give Jesus the honor He deserved. When He arrived in Galilee, some accepted Him only because they had seen His works in Jerusalem. The Samaritans believed without seeing, but the Jews needed to see before they believed.

<!-- segment: S0048 -->
Jesus’s mission was to save both the Jews and the Gentiles. Anyone lost in sin can call on the name of the Lord and be saved. Though the Jews ultimately rejected Jesus, He became a sacrifice for their sins. His sacrifice was for every person and group, including you.

# DAY FOUR

<!-- segment: S0053 -->
**PHILIPPIANS 4:6-7**

<!-- segment: S0054 -->
What is your definition of the word “anything”? As it is used in this Scripture, it encompasses everything; nothing is left out. Think back to this week’s lesson. The father was desperate for his son’s healing. When Jesus told him that his son was healed, the man believed, even though he hadn’t seen his son yet.

<!-- segment: S0055 -->
When you read, “Do not be anxious about anything,” your mind may go to a situation that is the exception. You may feel like your situation is beyond help, even from God. You may believe that God can work things out for other people, but your case is different, too difficult.

<!-- segment: S0056 -->
What Scripture says is true: No situation in your life is too far out of God’s reach for Him to take care of. Everything means *everything*. The Scripture goes on to say that you only need to turn over everything to God through prayer. He cares. He wants to meet you where you are. He wants to take the burden you are carrying and replace your anxiety with peace. When you let go of control and give it over to God, the peace that surpasses all understanding will cover your mind and dissolve the worry. Because of Jesus and with the help of the Holy Spirit, we have access to peace. You can’t do anything to earn it; instead, the God of all creation sees you, cares about you, and takes care of you. He sent Jesus to create a way for you to experience His peace. You must simply believe.

# DAY FIVE

<!-- segment: S0061 -->
**JOHN 4:51-53**

<!-- segment: S0062 -->
What is the most painful situation or season you have ever been in? Was finding meaning in it easy or difficult? Your hardest, most desperate situations can be used for good and for God’s glory. Saying we believe this is easy, but actually believing it is more difficult when you’re in the middle of desperate times. Those times test whether or not we believe what we claim. In today’s passage, the father learns that his son was healed in the same moment Jesus said he was healed. This moment could have easily been overlooked or missed. In today’s culture, we tend to believe that miracles like this don’t happen. How often do we miss the opportunity to point others to Jesus because we do not see Jesus’s work happening right in front of us?

<!-- segment: S0063 -->
This father didn’t miss it; he knew Jesus had healed his son. The most painful moment of his life was redeemed by Jesus, and he told everyone about it. We can use this story as a model for us to imitate: The father invited Jesus into a very desperate situation because he knew Jesus was the only One who could fix it. Then, he invited everyone else into the story so that they could see that Jesus could help them too.

<!-- segment: S0064 -->
What about you? What painful situation are you in right now, and how have you looked to Jesus to redeem it? Friend, Jesus redeeming the hardest seasons of your life is just as possible for you as it was for this young boy and his father. The next step for you is simple: Invite Jesus to fix it, and then tell anyone who will listen about His goodness.
