## THEOLOGY CONNECTION

<!-- segment: S0018 -->
### REGENERATION

<!-- segment: S0019 -->
Regeneration takes place at the beginning of the Christian life and is the miraculous transformation, or the new birth, that takes place within an individual through the supernatural work of the Holy Spirit (John 3:3-8; Titus 3:5). It is the divine side of conversion (a person turning to Christ in repentance and faith), being the work of God within a person’s life that causes him or her to be born again, a work that human effort is unable to produce.

## CULTURAL CONNECTION

<!-- segment: S0028 -->
You’ve probably messed up before and wanted to just start over. Maybe you made a horrible grade and wanted to retake a test. Maybe you gossiped about a friend, and you’d love to undo your words and the damage they caused. Maybe you sinned and did something you never thought you’d do, and you’d love to go back in time to make the right choice. In some situations, we can start over, but when it comes to our sin, we can’t. We can’t go back to the beginning and choose never to sin. Why? When Adam and Eve fell to sin, all of humanity was infected by it. We are born into a sinful world, and we can’t fix it. This may sound hopeless, but it just means that we needed someone else to fix it for us—someone never infected with sin. Jesus’s sacrifice provides something better than a second chance because, if given another chance, we’d squander it. Instead, we are reborn! When we call on Jesus for salvation, He gives us His Spirit. We are renewed, continuously covered by Jesus’s blood and no longer bound to our sin!

# DAY ONE

<!-- segment: S0030 -->
**NUMBERS 21:4-9**

<!-- segment: S0031 -->
Raise your hand if you can be stubborn! Let’s be honest, we want to prove to ourselves and to others that we can do what we think and say we can do. We can hit that new record in the gym and crush that ACT score. Some of us struggle to realize when we need help with something. The reality is that sometimes, we need someone else to step in for us, and that’s okay.

<!-- segment: S0032 -->
As much as we try, we can’t purify our hearts and minds. We need help from the only One who is capable of making us new: Jesus. So, God sent His Son down to earth to cleanse our hearts and minds—to give us new life (John 3:16). The problem with the heart is that it is deeply rooted in sin, going all the way back to the beginning—with the first humans, Adam and Eve. In this week’s lesson, Jesus referenced today’s passage. God’s people lost sight of all the Lord had done for them on the journey to the promised land. God saw Israel’s complaining and rebellion and sent poisonous snakes as a painful consequence of their distrust. But let’s not miss this important part: God made a way for them to heal. Restoration could happen only if they had the faith to look *up* at the bronze serpent. This act probably didn’t make sense to many of them, but they had to trust what God commanded if they wanted to be healed.

<!-- segment: S0033 -->
This cycle repeated for most of Israel’s history: freedom, rebellion, restoration. We repeat this cycle too. God freed us from sin, but we so easily forget and wander away from Him. You may feel ashamed, embarrassed, or even afraid to approach Him. But God sees you and loves you. He grants new life to each of us, but we must look up in faith!

# DAY TWO

<!-- segment: S0038 -->
**JEREMIAH 29:13**

<!-- segment: S0039 -->
Think about your favorite teachers. Maybe they were relaxed and funny. Maybe they helped you easily understand the subject they taught by keeping class fun and interesting. Maybe they gave extensions on really hard assignments. More than likely though, one reason they are your favorite is because they were approachable. They were easy to talk to because they listened. They tried to relate to what you were going through and always had your back because they wanted you to succeed. These are the great teachers.

<!-- segment: S0040 -->
Being approachable is one of the qualities that separates the one true God from false gods. God desires for us to come to Him; in fact, He has always made a way to be near to His people—through sacrifices, the ark of the covenant, the tabernacle, the temple, His Son, and now, through the Holy Spirit. Because God wants us to come to Him, He welcomes our struggles and questions. We saw this in the conversation between Jesus and Nicodemus in John 3. Every question Nicodemus asked drew him closer to recognizing Jesus’s identity as the Messiah. Jesus didn’t push away his questions but engaged him in conversation. Jesus revealed God’s character, and His character remains the same. When His people seek Him, God promises to respond. Jeremiah was writing to the Jews exiled in Babylon and spoke of a future, when they would be freed and return to their land. God hadn’t left them and promised that though they were exiled, He would restore them. Though we aren’t exiled in Babylon, these verses give us hope too. God desires a relationship with us, and when we seek Him, we will find Him. He isn’t hiding and wants us to find Him. Even when you feel like you’ve wandered too far from God, you can take a single step toward Him and know He will respond.

# DAY THREE

<!-- segment: S0045 -->
**JOHN 3:16-18**

<!-- segment: S0046 -->
We all mess up from time to time and make a wrong choice. When this happens, we face the consequences of our actions. Maybe you recently disobeyed your parents, and they grounded you or took your phone away for a few days. Hopefully, you learned a lesson and grew as a result of that wrong decision.

<!-- segment: S0047 -->
The reason we make poor choices is because our hearts are broken by sin. Only God can fix this problem, and He did this by sending His Son for us. Today’s passage includes a verse that is probably one of the most well-known verses in the Bible. Can you see which one it is? You probably chose John 3:16! The verse gives us hope and assurance of God’s love, but you must also read the next two verses to really get the point. If we believe in the Lord and trust in Him for salvation, we are not condemned, though we will still face consequences here on earth for our sins. But if we know Jesus, we are forgiven and set free from the bondage of sin and death.

<!-- segment: S0048 -->
This doesn’t mean we get a free pass to keep doing the wrong thing because we are forgiven. Our forgiveness should lead us to do the opposite because God is so good and so kind! Paul wrote, “God’s kindness is meant to lead you to repentance” (Rom. 2:4). When we sin against God, not only do we receive His love and forgiveness, but we also learn what it means to follow and obey Him. As we walk with Jesus, we desire Him more than sin! God’s love meets us and teaches us to desire Him. So, the next time you do the wrong thing, confess it to God, receive His love, learn from your mistake, and ask God to help you desire Him more.

# DAY FOUR

<!-- segment: S0053 -->
**2 CORINTHIANS 5:17**

<!-- segment: S0054 -->
Let’s go back to the story of Jesus and Nicodemus talking about being “born again” in John 3. Nicodemus asked questions that many of us may ask the first time we read this passage: “How can a man be born when he is old? Can he enter a second time into his mother’s womb and be born?” Nicodemus thought Jesus was talking about being born again physically, but Jesus was speaking about being born again spiritually.

<!-- segment: S0055 -->
The apostle Paul described this later as being a “new creation.” Paul chose these words because they carry a lot of weight! Think about it: If something is new, it stands out next to other things that are old, used, broken, and worn out. It has an extra shine and glow to it. The word *creation* then speaks to the beginning of something. If you have surrendered your life to Jesus, then you have been made new! You are not the person you used to be. You have Him living inside of you, convicting you of sin and forgiving you, leading and guiding you, and giving you an eternal purpose and hope. As a student, living in this reality can be really hard because so many distractions are fighting for your attention. Maybe it’s the temptation to be popular and liked by everyone, to get good grades, to never lose a game, to worry about who you’re going to go to the prom with or what you’re going to do after graduation. When we remember who we are in Christ and that He has made us new, then we’ll remember that we can trust Him with all the other details of our lives. You’re a new creation—a living, breathing testimony of God’s power to transform even the worst of us into something new!

# DAY FIVE

<!-- segment: S0060 -->
**1 JOHN 1:5-10**

<!-- segment: S0061 -->
While we create many of the distractions that pull us away from God by not prioritizing our relationship with Him, many of them are due to the darkness around us. Ever since sin entered the picture, our world has been broken. The world isn’t how God intended it to be—without sin, without sickness and pain, perfect, peaceful. One day Jesus will come back to make all things right, but until that day, we live in a dark world. Satan works hard to put lies in our minds, and the temptation to sin is everywhere.

<!-- segment: S0062 -->
We aren’t powerless! Not only is Jesus with us in the darkness, but He also overcame it already. So, we have a choice to make: Will we live as new creations and walk through life empowered by the Holy Spirit living within us, or will we cave to the darkness? Walking in the light means that you desire a closeness with God above anything else. You want to know Him more, align with His heart, and do what Jesus would do. Walking in the light is choosing godliness over the sin that you desperately want to commit. And it’s confessing your sin and embracing God’s grace when you do give in.

<!-- segment: S0063 -->
The best time to start walking in the light is each morning when you wake up. Giving God the beginning of your day will set the tone for the rest of your day. Think about your daily schedule. Once the day gets going, it doesn’t stop between school, extra activities, and maybe work. Prioritizing time with God at the beginning of your day helps you determine to walk in the light with Him, not giving darkness a chance!
