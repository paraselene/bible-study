### GOD IS HOLY

<!-- segment: S0019 -->
God’s holiness refers to His uniqueness in being separate from all He has created. The Hebrew word for “holy” means “separate” or “set apart.” God’s holiness also refers to His absolute purity. God is unstained by the evil of the world. His goodness is perfect, and the moral code we find in the Scriptures is a reflection of His holy nature. As people made in God’s image, we are called to holiness.

## CULTURAL CONNECTION

<!-- segment: S0028 -->
What is worship? How do we worship God the right way? We use the word “worship” a lot in Christian culture, but the word even bleeds into secular, or worldly, culture. We say things like, “She worships the ground he walks on” and make people, brands, and activities our idols. Only God is worthy of worship, but we often bow before lesser “gods”—false gods that can’t save us, gods that lead us away from the one true God and to destruction. Worship is giving God the glory and praise He deserves; worship involves our words, but even more, worship is bowing our hearts before the Lord and living in submission to Him. Here’s how we know we’re worshiping God the right way: We examine what our love and adoration point to; we examine what we’re devoted to; we ask if our heart is divided between God and anything else. And if it is, we lay that thing down in surrender. Worship requires us to surrender our hearts, minds, and actions to the only One who is worthy.

# DAY ONE

<!-- segment: S0030 -->
**MATTHEW 23:23-26**

<!-- segment: S0031 -->
If you could have one superpower, what would it be? A lot of really cool options are available to choose from, like being invisible, being able to fly, traveling through time, or having super strength. What about mind reading, though? It may sound cool at first, but when you start thinking about it, would we really want to know what people are thinking?

<!-- segment: S0032 -->
The only person who can read our minds and see our hearts is Jesus. He knows each person inside and out. What’s incredible is that He still loves us, even when our thoughts are sinful, selfish, and ugly. Jesus doesn’t look for perfection in us; He looks for faithfulness. In today’s Scripture, we see a picture of people who were obsessed with looking perfect, but who didn’t truly love God and love people. They loved self-image more than their neighbor. So, Jesus called them out. He was able to do this because He is God. He saw through the people’s exterior and straight to their hearts. He sees through our attempts to look the part too.

<!-- segment: S0033 -->
Jesus wants His children to bring their imperfections to Him. When we do that, we show a desire for God to transform our hearts and our minds. Many of us may not come to God with honesty because we feel ashamed on the inside. But God can handle it! We can put our trust in Him, and He will take care of the rest. You may be tempted to stay away from God because of shame you feel over something you’ve done. God wants to heal your heart; He wants to transform you and give you peace. God sees you and loves you. No matter how ugly your heart and mind are, you can go to God in honesty and ask Him to make you new.

**ISAIAH 1:16-18**

<!-- segment: S0039 -->
The kitchen table is meant to be the place in the house for eating meals. On occasion though, we might eat on the couch or in our bed. We’ve probably all spilled our food or drink and ruined a good piece of furniture too. Cleaning up the mess usually takes more than some hot water and a washcloth and requires the best cleaning supplies we can find because nothing else will do the trick.

<!-- segment: S0040 -->
Our hearts are a lot like that mess we made from the food we spilled. Because of our sin, our hearts are stained beyond what we can fix. In fact, nothing can fix it—not our good works, moral living, or serving others. Jesus is the only One who can clean and remove the stain of sin for good. Today’s verses in Isaiah speak about the cleansing work Jesus does. As we discussed yesterday, Jesus sees the inside of our hearts and diagnoses our problem. He doesn’t leave us in that diagnosis without hope for healing. When we bring our sin problem to Him with honesty and desperation, He wipes it away. We receive a clean slate and are able to live the way God intended us to live.

<!-- segment: S0041 -->
That’s the power of the gospel message! The sacrifice Jesus made on the cross is more than enough to clean our sinful stains. When He died and shed His blood for us, He took on all the sins of the world. Then, He appeared to hundreds of people and went to heaven. He sent His Holy Spirit to live in us so we can be clean and live with new purpose! Thank you, Jesus, for making us clean!

# DAY THREE

<!-- segment: S0046 -->
**HEBREWS 4:1-10**

<!-- segment: S0047 -->
There is nothing like having a Saturday to sleep in, watch TV, and enjoy some good breakfast. After a long week of school and extra activities, we need those Saturdays at times. Actually, we need them more than we think! God created us to work, but He also created us for rest. They both matter, and one doesn’t work well without the other. We have all felt the tension of work and rest; we’ve all experienced fatigue that comes from living without balance.

<!-- segment: S0048 -->
This idea of work and rest also connects to our spiritual lives. God’s people in the Old Testament felt this tension too. Before Jesus came to earth, God’s people had to keep the law and make sacrifices for their sins. Can you imagine how tired God’s people must have been at times? Maybe even how they worried if they were doing it “right” or doing “enough”? God still provided them rest by giving them a weekly sabbath. The word Sabbath means to cease and stop. God still commands us to cease and stop all our work to just rest. But rest doesn’t come easy for a lot of us. We struggle to stop; some of us avoid pausing because doing so allows us time to think, and maybe there are things we don’t want to think about. Maybe we find too much satisfaction in our work. But God intended for us to rest. Our physical rest points to the spiritual rest God offers us through His Son, Jesus. The book of Hebrews was written for Christians who were used to earning righteousness through following the law. The people needed to receive the spiritual rest Jesus brought through the cross and resurrection. They could stop trying so hard and striving for righteousness because Jesus fulfilled the requirements of the law and earned their rest. He offers us rest too. The weekly Sabbath is a reminder that we don’t have to work for our salvation. We can rest in what Jesus did.

# DAY FOUR

<!-- segment: S0053 -->
**HEBREWS 13:15-16**

<!-- segment: S0054 -->
Sometimes churches use the word worship only when referring to a service on a Sunday morning or at youth group, a retreat, or at camp—when music plays, people sing, and someone teaches from the Bible. Although that’s not wrong, worship is much more than a service and singing songs. Because many of us are used to hearing worship described this way, we limit worship. Worship is actually a lifestyle! We worship who or what our thoughts and attention focus on the most. The question isn’t if we will worship but who or what will we worship?

<!-- segment: S0055 -->
Today’s passage tells us to offer a continual sacrifice of praise to God. At times, praise comes easily for us; at other times, praising God can feel like a sacrifice because life hurts. In those seasons, we honor God by giving Him glory despite walking through suffering or disappointment. Our sacrificial worship pleases God because we were made to give Him glory—when life is good and when life is painful. Why? Because God’s goodness never changes and isn’t dependent on our circumstances. So, how can we do this? First, we can worship God with our words, consistently expressing gratitude for who He is and what He has done for us. When you talk to others, you can tell them how Jesus gave you new life and purpose. Next, we can praise Him by being kind and generous with people in need. Worshiping with our words and our actions is like a spiritual offering, showing that Jesus has changed our hearts. When we praise God, we take the attention off of ourselves and direct it to Him. We celebrate His goodness and His glory.

# DAY FIVE

<!-- segment: S0060 -->
**ISAIAH 1:11-15; 6:1-8**

<!-- segment: S0061 -->
What’s the difference between doing what’s right and having a right heart? We can do something good for others and appear to care deeply for them while really hating them in our hearts. We can fool others when we do good works for all the wrong reasons: to get attention, to please others, to earn forgiveness.

<!-- segment: S0062 -->
The people of Judah looked like they were doing all the right things, but God saw past their actions to the real problem in their hearts. The prophet Isaiah wrote to the people of Judah, who had lived a double life for years and years. We see in chapter 1 just how bad the situation was. Although God had not abandoned His people, they had abandoned Him over and over. Because of this, God allowed the destruction of their temple and their city. God desired for His people to be made new. In chapter 6, Isaiah encountered God and saw his own sin in all its heaviness and ugliness. He saw God for who He was too—holy. This encounter with the holy God changed him forever. God then told Isaiah to go and preach to Judah. Judah had a heart problem. Because their hearts weren’t clean, their worship wasn’t meaningful to God and fell short of what God demanded—of what He deserved. Isaiah confronted this problem but also assured the people that a Messiah would come to cleanse their hearts for good.

<!-- segment: S0063 -->
Does your heart resemble Judah’s? Does your worship fall short of what God demands and deserves, which is everything? Confess this to God. Receive His grace. If you do, God will send you out with great purpose. You’ll never be the same!
