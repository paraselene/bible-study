## CULTURAL CONNECTION

<!-- segment: S0028 -->
Does the idea of sharing your faith make you nervous? Do you freeze? Do you worry how people will react if you talk about Jesus in class, on your teams, or with your friends? If you do, you aren’t alone. When we encounter Jesus, He changes us. Some of those changes are drastic and immediate, while some happen more slowly, as God continues to work in us. But the people who knew us before we knew Jesus may not understand the change. Instead of being up front about who Jesus is and what He has done in our lives, we stay quiet. Why? We worry more about people’s opinions of us than their salvation. We worry that they’ll think we’re strange, radical, or judgmental. But if the gospel is really the best news—if we’ve been saved from sin and death—how can we keep silent? How can we not share the best news with others?

# DAY ONE

<!-- segment: S0030 -->
**1 KINGS 19:19-21**

<!-- segment: S0031 -->
In the verses leading up to today’s passage, God told Elijah to anoint Elisha to be the next prophet in his place. Elijah calling Elisha to join him in ministerial service gives us an example from the Old Testament of what it looks like to give up everything to follow someone. When Jesus walked on earth and invited men and women to follow Him as disciples, Jews would have remembered Elisha being called and understood the weight of the call. The call to follow Jesus is not a casual invitation to be acquaintances with Him but a strong call to live with Him, walk with Him, and forsake all else to follow Him.

<!-- segment: S0032 -->
This story of Elisha paints a picture of what it means to give up everything for something much greater. Choosing to follow Jesus comes at a great cost. When Elijah found Elisha, Elisha was working in the fields with twelve pairs of oxen, which symbolized Elisha’s great wealth. The call to follow Elijah meant that Elisha must walk away from wealth, material possessions, his way of life, and even his family, but Elisha considered following Elijah worth it. How much more should we as believers consider following Jesus as worthy, even when it costs us our life, our popularity, our wealth, and our own desires? No matter what it costs us, we can trust that Jesus is always better. Following Jesus comes at a cost, but we will study this week that not following Him is more costly. Do you believe God is worthy of your whole life? Then trust Him and make Him the most important thing in your life. Do you believe God is worth it to follow all the days of your life? Then live like He is worthy.

# DAY TWO

<!-- segment: S0037 -->
**MATTHEW 16:24-26**

<!-- segment: S0038 -->
These words of Jesus continue to help us form our understanding of how much it will cost us to truly follow Him. While the invitation to follow Jesus is absolutely extended to everyone, it comes with some costly requirements: Deny yourself, take up your cross, and follow Him. Denying yourself is intentionally turning away from selfish or worldly living. Taking up your cross is committing to endure whatever comes in this life, knowing Jesus is always worth any cost or suffering. Finally, following Jesus is not being casually familiar with Jesus, but wholeheartedly trusting Him, conforming to His ways instead of the world’s ways, and remaining steadfast even when life gets hard.

<!-- segment: S0039 -->
If you spend your life clinging to worldly comforts, you will lose everything in the end. But if you die to your worldly desires and live for God instead, you will find eternal life. New, abundant, eternal life is guaranteed for every believer who gives up the worldly way of living to follow Jesus; it’s as simple as that! But something being simple doesn’t make it easy. Knowing that God walks with us and gives us strength to die to ourselves and live for Him gives us great hope. If you want to find real life here on earth and for all of eternity, then it’s time to put your selfish desires to death and live for Someone so much greater. Following Jesus is a narrow road that many will not choose, but that doesn’t mean it’s not worth it in the end. Jesus’s death and resurrection give us the promise that while death may be part of the story, it is never the end of the story for His followers. In His kingdom, death to sin always leads to new life. Will you decide that Jesus is worth following no matter what you have to give up?

# DAY THREE

<!-- segment: S0044 -->
**MATTHEW 6:25-34**

<!-- segment: S0045 -->
Following Jesus brings peace, joy, and fulfillment that we can never know apart from Him. But this decision can also come with moments of rejection, loneliness, and fear. What if my friends stop hanging out with me? What if my family makes fun of me or feels offended when I try to share the gospel with them? What if people view me differently? What if I stop getting invited? What if I mess this up? These are just a few examples of how doubts, fears, and what-ifs can enter our minds and spiral into worry that keeps us distracted and stuck. The words of Jesus in Matthew 6 remind us that only when we listen and obey Him will we find peace and a cure for the anxious feelings that cause us to spiral. Worries and fears will pop into your head and show up in your life; the question is what will you do with them? Will you choose to lean into that fear and stay stuck, or will you choose to live by faith, running to God with every worry?

<!-- segment: S0046 -->
To live from a place of fear and worry is to live a life that is distracted from the abundant life God has for you. Jesus gives us a better way in verse 33: “Seek first the kingdom.” Other verses in the Bible teach similar ideas, commanding us to set our minds on things above (Col. 3:2) and to focus on what is unseen and eternal (2 Cor 4:18). We are commanded to intentionally and consistently focus our minds and hearts on Jesus alone. When we feel tempted to wander into worry, we confess that to God and redirect our attention to Him. And we practice that over and over again. Following Jesus comes with uncertainty; we may not always know how our lives will play out, but He promises us His presence and His peace for every step of the journey!

# DAY FOUR

<!-- segment: S0051 -->
**JOHN 1:43-49**

<!-- segment: S0052 -->
Toward the end of John 1, we read about multiple encounters where Jesus invited people to follow Him as His disciples. A few of the first disciples to follow Jesus were brothers named Andrew and Peter and a man named Phillip from Bethsaida. When Jesus invited Phillip to follow Him, Phillip not only decided to follow Jesus, but he also couldn’t keep it to himself. He went to Nathanael to share the news that he had met Jesus—the promised Messiah prophesied about for centuries—and to invite Nathanael to meet Him too. What a beautiful picture of how we are also called to follow Jesus and then spend our lives inviting others to meet this same, life-changing Messiah for themselves.

<!-- segment: S0053 -->
Nathanael went with Phillip to meet Jesus. When Jesus saw Nathanael coming, He said, “Behold, an Israelite indeed, in whom there is no deceit” (v. 47), demonstrating His supernatural ability to know our hearts. Nathanael confusedly asked, “How do you know me?” (v. 48). Jesus answered his question with a powerful truth about who Jesus is. Jesus explained to Nathanael that He already knew him before his friend Phillip ever went to find him under the fig tree. This brings us to a second beautiful picture of the gospel—before we ever knew or went looking for God, He knew us and looked for us. In other words, God created you, saw you, loved you, and pursued you before you were ever aware of Him. As followers of Jesus, we must remember where God brought us from and how He pursued us. And may we share our personal testimony with others so that they, too, can find God, who has always been pursuing them.

# DAY FIVE

<!-- segment: S0058 -->
**PHILIPPIANS 3:7-9**

<!-- segment: S0059 -->
Gold is a precious metal that is recognized as a valuable treasure. Fool’s gold, on the other hand, is shiny like gold and looks like gold but has no value. In a similar way, our achievements and worldly treasures may shine and look impressive, but they have zero eternal value. Knowing Jesus personally, however, is like real gold—a precious treasure that is eternally and immeasurably valuable. In the verses leading up to today’s passage, Paul basically explained that he had what the world would recognize as an impressive list of achievements and titles. Then, he takes a sharp turn to say that the whole list is nothing compared to the value he found in following Christ. He even goes so far as to say that he now considers those things to be to be rubbish. Yes, you read that right; Paul described everything that he once considered valuable to now be manure, garbage, and waste.

<!-- segment: S0060 -->
As we have studied this week, following Jesus does cost us. Paul’s words in this passage remind us that even though the cost is great, we find a far greater reward in following Jesus. Does Paul’s testimony that Jesus is better than everything else mean that he never faced hardship in following Jesus? Absolutely not. Paul suffered persecution, imprisonment, and even violent death, but something being easy is different than something being worth it. For Paul, knowing Jesus intimately was better than anything and everything he had once considered valuable; his relationship with Jesus was his greatest joy and source of fulfillment. Jesus invites us to do the same—to give up worldly “treasures” that might seem shiny but have no lasting value and to treasure the valuable, eternal gift of relationship with Him.
