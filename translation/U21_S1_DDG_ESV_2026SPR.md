## THEOLOGY CONNECTION

<!-- segment: S0018 -->
### MIRACLES

<!-- segment: S0019 -->
A miracle is an event in which God makes an exception to the natural order of things, or defies natural laws, for the purpose of demonstrating His glory and/or validating His message. Miracles are recorded throughout Scripture; miraculous signs and wonders were often evident when a prophet or an apostle was speaking God’s message to the people. Because we believe God to be all-powerful and personally involved in this world, we believe He can and does perform miracles.

## CULTURAL CONNECTION

<!-- segment: S0028 -->
Have you ever promised to do better—to be a better person, friend, son, or daughter? Or maybe you wanted to be a better athlete or student, a better brother or sister. Our efforts to be better often fall short. We try for a while, but we revert back to old patterns and behaviors. The truth is that we *can’t* be better on our own. When it comes to sin, we can’t do better at all. Our efforts to be good will come up short. The more we try to be good enough apart from Jesus, the more disappointed and ashamed we’ll feel. The good news is that Jesus doesn’t just help us be better; Jesus makes us *new* (2 Cor. 5:17). He changes us into something we’re not. This transformation is a beautiful exchange that none of us deserve; we receive Jesus’s righteousness in exchange for Him taking our sin. So, can you be a better person, friend, daughter, son? No, Jesus makes you even better than better; He makes you *new*.

# DAY ONE

<!-- segment: S0030 -->
**PSALM 145**

<!-- segment: S0031 -->
When we read through the miracles of Jesus in the Gospels, we see Jesus meet two kinds of needs: physical and spiritual. Miracles show us that only Jesus can meet both. This is a big deal because being physical and spiritual is what makes us human. God knows this because He made us, so only He can care for us completely. He cares about our eternal life, but He also cares about the here and now.

<!-- segment: S0032 -->
We don’t just see this truth in the Gospels but also in the Old Testament. Look again at verses 14-19 and pay attention to how David described God’s love and care for us. The actions that God takes toward us are unbelievable! Each word in these verses is filled with so much intention and purpose. God helps us, sees us, feeds us, and satisfies us. He is a good and giving God!

<!-- segment: S0033 -->
God is always reaching toward us. Are we receiving His love and walking in it? The way God works in our lives can be a mystery. On our worst days, we may take our frustration out on God; we may accuse Him of not meeting our needs. But God never leaves us, and His care for us is constant. Other people and situations in life will let us down, but God always remains the same, regardless of how bad life gets. He sees the big picture and is working a greater, eternal plan. God’s goodness and faithfulness should lead us to praise Him and speak about Him to others, just as David did.

**1 KINGS 17:1-7**

<!-- segment: S0039 -->
One of the most beautiful things about being a Christian is that you can rest when the world can’t. When you catch up on the latest news, you probably aren’t encouraged. For the most part, the news showcases the bad things that are happening around the world. There may be the occasional update that is positive, but for the most part, the news shows a picture of unrest.

<!-- segment: S0040 -->
Yesterday’s passage reminded us that God truly cares for us, even when life may not make sense in the moment. One way that God cares for His children is by giving us what we need when complete chaos is raging around us. Let’s put ourselves in the shoes of the prophet Elijah for a minute. God used Elijah to speak God’s truth to His people who were worshiping the false god Baal. God’s own people, the nation of Israel, strayed away from Him and gave into the chaos of false worship. They followed cultural norms instead of God’s truth. God gave Elijah a message to deliver to Israel’s king: A drought and famine would come to Israel because of their disobedience.

<!-- segment: S0041 -->
Even though Elijah was outnumbered, God took care of him because Elijah was faithful. Even though Israel would be hungry and thirsty, Elijah wouldn’t be. The way God took care of Elijah is incredible! So, when those around you seem to be caving into chaos, you have the option to rest because God cares for you and always works for your good. Keep your eyes on God and know He will never leave you.

**1 KINGS 17:8-16**

<!-- segment: S0047 -->
Wants and needs are completely different. You may want those new shoes to show off to your friends, but you definitely don’t need them. Wanting nice shoes or the newest phone isn’t bad, but when we obsess over new things and think we need them, we are heading the wrong way!

<!-- segment: S0048 -->
God knows exactly what we need and when we need it. He knows what we can handle in each season of our lives. Although we should bring every desire that we have to God, we should also trust Him with the results. Can you imagine what this world would be like if every single person got what they wanted every time they asked? Talk about a disaster waiting to happen! Thankfully, God helps us with this.

<!-- segment: S0049 -->
Yesterday we read from 1 Kings 17:1-7 about Israel experiencing a drought and famine because of their worship of the false god, Baal. The one person who was spared from all of this was Elijah. God then continued to provide for Elijah. As he journeyed, a widow gave him some bread and water. It was just what he needed for the next step along the way. God would make sure Elijah’s needs were being met, but he would do so in a way that required Elijah to remain completely dependent on Him for every bite of food and drink of water. Elijah would experience God’s hand in each step of the journey.

<!-- segment: S0050 -->
Sometimes God provides for us in huge ways, where a breakthrough seems to happen and everything changes. Other times, He gives us just what we need in a moment, so that we can learn contentment and remember to trust Him. How will you trust Him in either situation?

# DAY FOUR

<!-- segment: S0055 -->
**PHILIPPIANS 4:10-13**

<!-- segment: S0056 -->
Most of us know what it feels like to really want something; many of us also know what it feels like to have more than enough. Do we know how it feels to be content, though? Contentment is satisfaction with what you have, not desiring more or less.

<!-- segment: S0057 -->
One of the greatest examples of contentment in the Bible comes from the apostle Paul. Before Paul met Jesus, he had quite the resume. Not only was he extremely smart, but he was also in charge of a lot of people and heavily mistreated Christians. Then one day, his life changed forever for the good! Jesus appeared to him in a miraculous way, and Paul surrendered his life to Him. Paul then went on a life-long journey to spread the gospel across the known world at that time. Paul had a radically different view of life, so much so, that he described his past accomplishments as if they didn’t matter anymore. You can read about this in Philippians 3. Everything about Paul changed because that’s what Jesus does: He transforms us and makes us new!

<!-- segment: S0058 -->
Paul wrote this letter to the church at Philippi from prison. He had experienced the highest of highs in life. Remember that resume? Paul had reached the height of his career and leadership, from a worldly sense, but from a prison cell, none of his worldly accomplishments mattered. Christ was enough! As a student, a lot of worldly desires try to steal your contentment, whether on a screen, in a relationship, with your grades, or in your athletic goals. You’ll struggle to be content when you’re constantly comparing what you have or don’t have to others. When your satisfaction is in Christ, then you can be content when those other things aren’t going well. When you build your life on Christ, then and only then, will you be content.

# DAY FIVE

<!-- segment: S0063 -->
**EXODUS 7:14-25**

<!-- segment: S0064 -->
Behind every gift is a giver. What’s your favorite gift you received at Christmas or on your birthday? You may have asked for this gift and had a feeling that you might get it, or maybe you were completely surprised. When you opened the gift, you were locked in on that moment. Full of excitement and anticipation, you were ready to enjoy that gift. Hopefully before you used the gift, you recognized and thanked the person who gave it to you. If it wasn’t for that person, the gift wouldn’t be yours, right?

<!-- segment: S0065 -->
Throughout Scripture, we read about God giving good gifts to His people, sometimes in unexpected ways. In Exodus, we see God’s wondrous work through Moses, as he embarked on the journey to free Israel from Egyptian slavery. God’s provision for His people came in many ways, but one way was through bringing plagues on the Egyptians. The Nile River was crucial to Egyptian survival and to their way of life. Pharaoh had previously thrown Hebrew male infants into the Nile as a method of cruel control (Ex. 1:22). But through Moses’s obedience, God changed the Nile River to blood as judgment on Egypt for mistreating His people. This act of provision for Israel’s release from slavery points to the ultimate provision of Jesus releasing us from slavery to sin. Many years later, Jesus transformed water again, this time into crimson wine—blessing instead of judgment. Do you see the connection? Jesus reversed the curse of judgment through His own blood, and this miracle at the wedding in Cana hinted at His perfect sacrifice. The water turned to wine foreshadowed that Jesus would provide for our deepest need—forgiveness for sin and freedom from judgment!
