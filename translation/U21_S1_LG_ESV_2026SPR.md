## Context

<!-- segment: S0031 -->
Today’s passage is full of Jewish cultural implications that are sometimes difficult for modern readers to understand. Ancient Jewish weddings were significant moments in a community, and the celebration lasted up to an entire week. Families were expected to provide for guests; running out of food, drinks, and so on brought shame on the family, and in some cases, lawsuits for not honoring the traditional agreement! Mary asking Jesus to help reflected these cultural expectations. Modern readers are also alarmed at Jesus calling Mary “woman,” but the term wasn’t disrespectful. Jesus used this same term during the crucifixion when He asked John to care for her as his own mother (John 19:26). Many scholars believe Jesus’s conversation with Mary revealed His commitment to do the Father’s will first, even when what He willed was in opposition to opinions of Jesus’s loved ones. Rather than immediately agreeing to perform a miracle, Jesus confirmed the time was appropriate with His Father. Though the hour for Jesus’s death hadn’t come, the time for His public ministry had, and His first miracle pointed to His final hour.

### Theology Connection

<!-- segment: S0033 -->
#### Miracles

<!-- segment: S0034 -->
A miracle is an event in which God makes an exception to the natural order of things, or defies natural laws, for the purpose of demonstrating His glory and/or validating His message. Miracles are recorded throughout Scripture; miraculous signs and wonders were often evident when a prophet or an apostle was speaking God’s message to the people. Because we believe God to be all-powerful and personally involved in this world, we believe He can and does perform miracles.

## Discussion

<!-- segment: S0050 -->
**Main Point:** Jesus meets our needs to help us see Him and know Him.

<!-- segment: S0051 -->
### Jesus Provides Something Better

<!-- segment: S0052 -->
**Read John 2:1-8**

<!-- segment: S0053 -->
**WOULD PEOPLE TODAY EXPECT JESUS TO BE INVITED TO A FUN EVENT? WHAT DOES JESUS BEING INVITED SHOW US?**

<!-- segment: S0054 -->
We often have a skewed view of Jesus—maybe because of how shows and movies depict Him or because we don’t have a deep understanding of Jewish culture. Jesus is often portrayed as always serious, always pointing out sin, never having fun. But Jesus was fully human, and based on passages like this and others where crowds and children flocked to Him, we get a better glimpse of who He was: **Jesus radiated love and joy.** People were drawn to Him. Jesus didn’t separate Himself from people; He had friends! He celebrated their joyful occasions and spent time with them.

<!-- segment: S0055 -->
**WHAT DID MARY’S INSTRUCTIONS REVEAL ABOUT HER VIEW OF JESUS (V. 5)? WHAT ROLE DID THE SERVERS’ OBEDIENCE PLAY IN THE MIRACLE?**

<!-- segment: S0056 -->
Jesus didn’t answer Mary directly, but she trusted the outcome to Him. After bringing the problem to her son, Mary felt the freedom to walk away. **What if we trusted Jesus so much that we rested in His ability to handle *every part* of our lives?** The servers did what Jesus asked and simply filled the jars. Their small act of obedience opened the door for miraculous transformation and played a huge role in Jesus’s first miracle. **Like Mary, we can trust Jesus; like the servers, our obedience matters more than we know.**

<!-- segment: S0057 -->
**WHAT WAS THE JARS’ PURPOSE (V. 6)? HOW DOES KNOWING THEIR PURPOSE HELP US UNDERSTAND THE *MEANING* OF THE MIRACLE?**

<!-- segment: S0058 -->
The six jars of water were in place for guests to cleanse themselves before eating, not for drinking. The jars were part of the Jews’ purity rituals. Jesus changed their purpose, though. Let’s zero in on the depth of this first miracle and the radical statement it conveyed. Jews ritually and physically cleansed themselves with water. But water could never cleanse them, or us, internally. Jesus’s blood washes us clean from our sin. He purifies us internally, which is then reflected externally by how we live—in our actions, our words, our thoughts. **Jesus’s first miracle pointed to a greater miracle: The temporary cleansing that the law and the sacrificial system provided would be fulfilled in Him! Jesus’s sacrifice ushered in a way to be cleansed permanently by calling on Him for salvation.** This miracle was a sign that the new covenant was coming—the one God promised would change us from the inside out (Jer. 31:33-34) and the one that would provide “rivers of living water” deep within us through the Holy Spirit (John 7:38). The water in these jars pointed to the great salvation that was on the way!

<!-- segment: S0059 -->
### Jesus’s Miracles Reveal His Identity

<!-- segment: S0060 -->
**Read John 2:9-11**

<!-- segment: S0061 -->
**HOW DOES THIS MIRACLE HELP YOU SEE THAT JESUS CARES ABOUT MORE THAN JUST OUR NEEDS? HOW HAVE YOU SEEN HIM WORK IN SMALL WAYS IN YOUR LIFE?**

<!-- segment: S0062 -->
This wedding party probably seems insignificant to most of us. The party ended, and everyone went home. Was the need for more wine really that big of a deal? Well, outside of the Jews’ cultural expectations that meant the family would be looked down on (and maybe even treated as outcasts or sued) for not providing for guests, no. They could’ve survived without it, but Jesus revealed His identity through meeting a desire. **He cares deeply about us, a care extending beyond our needs only. Jesus works in seemingly insignificant moments in our lives too.** When we have spiritual eyes to notice His work, then we gain a deeper understanding of His love and His character.

<!-- segment: S0063 -->
**WHY DID JESUS PERFORM MIRACLES? DO WE STILL TRUST GOD TO DO MIRACLES TODAY? HAVE YOU SEEN ANY?**

<!-- segment: S0064 -->
This miracle was the first of many Jesus performed, but every miracle served the similar purpose of revealing Jesus’s identity. At the wedding in Cana, Jesus didn’t just supply a little more wine to get the family through the party. He had the servers fill the jars to the brim. They overflowed with new wine—with the very *best* wine. Jesus transformed water into something else entirely. **The miracle pointed to something greater: Jesus gave abundantly of Himself, of His blood, for us. His sacrifice was so pure, so perfect, that it was enough to cover all sin for all time, like a fountain that never stops and is overflowing (Zech. 13:1).** Miracles still happen; God still heals and restores. As believers, we are evidence of His transforming power.

<!-- segment: S0065 -->
**HOW DID THE MIRACLE AFFECT JESUS’S NEW FOLLOWERS?**

<!-- segment: S0066 -->
After witnessing the miracle, Jesus’s new disciples believed in Him (v. 11). The Jews knew God’s promise to send another prophet like Moses (Deut. 18:15). Maybe the miracle mirrored the first plague, when God, through Moses, changed the Nile River to blood as judgment on Egypt (Ex. 7:19). **Jesus, the greater Moses, changed water to wine—a symbol of joy and abundance.** Jesus provided wine at a literal wedding, but He also provided everything we need for another wedding—one to which He and Scripture both point (Rev. 19:7-8). The Bible refers to the church as the Bride and Christ as the Bridegroom. **We are clothed in white, cleansed and prepared for this great wedding instead of the judgment we deserve.**

## Wrap-Up

<!-- segment: S0068 -->
**GOD CAN CHANGE WATER INTO SOMETHING ELSE ENTIRELY, SO WHY DO WE STRUGGLE TO TRUST HIM WITH BIG AND SMALL DETAILS OF OUR LIVES?**

<!-- segment: S0069 -->
**When we read about Jesus’s miracles and the great wonders God did in the Old Testament, we often separate them from our own lives. We believe these accounts are true; we just doubt that God can or will work like this for us. When Jesus changed water to wine, He didn’t just add something to the water; He transformed it entirely and made it something it wasn’t before. God does the same thing with your heart, changing it from stone to flesh and helping you to follow Him (Ezek. 11:19). He gives you the Holy Spirit, empowering you to live for Him in a way you can’t do on your own. This truth **_is_** a miracle. Every single day God works a miracle in your heart by living within you! Every breath you inhale and exhale is a miracle. We sometimes forget that the God of all creation shares His Spirit with us. God works all around you and within you. He uses the big stuff in your life, of course. But He uses the small stuff too. God has only ever been faithful. You can trust God, who has transformed you, with every part of your life—big and small.**
