### Biblical Commentary

<!-- segment: S0020 -->
#### When We Find Jesus, We Tell Others

<!-- segment: S0021 -->
**John 1:35-37**

<!-- segment: S0022 -->
“Having directed a general witness to his people Israel, John now offered that same witness specifically to his disciples. It is one thing to state a sweeping conviction to the masses, quite another to deliberately give up one’s own devotees; but John did not flinch from dismantling the rapidly growing movement that had brought him fame and followers (3:22-30). It was a mark of his effectiveness as a witness that although two disciples heard John speak, they followed Jesus. Soon they would be attributing to him the very titles of ultimate honor which John had renounced for himself (vv. 20,41).”¹⁵

<!-- segment: S0023 -->
**John 1:43**

<!-- segment: S0024 -->
“The call of Philip as recorded here has been reduced to its most essential element: Follow me. This phrase also lies at the core of the call to commitment in the Synoptic Gospels (Mark 1:17; 2:14; 8:34; 10:21). It defines discipleship in relational rather than in intellectual, emotional, or actional terms. Philip was not offered an idea to ponder, a mood to experience, or a task to accomplish, but a person to obey. In place of law in the sacred Scriptures or ritual at the holy Temple, Jesus dared to identify himself as the source of guidance to God. Furthermore, his summons was future-facing. Following implies an open-ended, dynamic pilgrimage, not a closed, static position. Christianity is not a place to stand but a road to walk in choice companionship with the ‘leader’ of life (Acts 3:15; Heb. 12:2).”¹⁶

<!-- segment: S0025 -->
#### Jesus Sees Us and Knows Us

<!-- segment: S0026 -->
**John 1:51**

<!-- segment: S0027 -->
“What will they see? In Genesis 28 Jacob falls asleep and has a dream. In this dream he sees a ladder that extends from heaven to earth. Angels are using this ladder to move between the two. Above the ladder stood the Lord God. Jesus is the ladder to God. He is the only way into God’s presence. He promises Nathanael he will recognize that Jesus alone can bring a person into God’s presence.”¹⁷

## Context

<!-- segment: S0029 -->
In only seventeen verses, John revealed seven names for Jesus, making sure his readers understood Jesus’s identity: (1) “Lamb of God,” who atoned for sin; (2) “Rabbi,” meaning “teacher”; (3) “Messiah” and “Christ,” both meaning “Anointed One”; (4) “son of Joseph,” fully human and of David’s lineage; (5) “Son of God” and (6) “King of Israel,” both fulfilling prophecies; (7) “Son of Man,” the One Daniel envisioned (Dan. 7:13). These names encompass Jesus’s identity and His purpose: He is the One all of Scripture pointed to as the coming Savior King. Jesus is the perfect sacrificial Lamb; the One who teaches us how to know the Father and how to follow His commands; the Anointed One *and* one of us; God’s only Son and promised King from David’s line; the eternal Son of Man, who is worthy of approaching the Ancient of Days (Dan. 7:13) and receiving a “dominion and glory and a kingdom, that all peoples, nations, and languages should serve him” (Dan. 7:14). Amen!

#### Evangelism

<!-- segment: S0032 -->

Every child of God has the duty to seek constantly to win the lost to Christ by verbal witness and by a Christian lifestyle.

Each group is different, so adapt these activities to fit your group’s needs. The goal is for students to connect more to the lesson as they connect to the topic and to one another.

### 1. Celebrating the Good

<!-- segment: S0042 -->
**Supplies List: None**

<!-- segment: S0043 -->
Ask the following questions, allowing time for students to share responses:

<!-- segment: S0044 -->
- **WHEN SOMETHING GOOD OR EXCITING HAPPENS FOR YOU, WHO IS THE FIRST PERSON YOU TELL? WHY?**
- **HOW DOES IT FEEL WHEN SOMEONE IS GENUINELY EXCITED ABOUT THE GOOD THINGS THAT HAPPEN IN YOUR LIFE?**
- **HOW DOES IT FEEL WHEN YOU SHARE SOMETHING MEANINGFUL BUT OTHERS DON’T CARE OR SUPPORT YOU?**

<!-- segment: S0045 -->
Having people to celebrate our good news makes the news feel even more exciting. In the same way that we run to these people to share our excitement, we’re called to run and tell others about Jesus. But instead, we often keep this news quiet. Today, we’ll learn that knowing Jesus as Savior should prompt us to share the gospel.

<!-- segment: S0046 -->
### 2. Cultural Connection

<!-- segment: S0047 -->
Does the idea of sharing your faith make you nervous? Do you freeze? Do you worry how people will react if you talk about Jesus in class, on your teams, or with your friends? If you do, you aren’t alone. When we encounter Jesus, He changes us. Some of those changes are drastic and immediate, while some happen more slowly, as God continues to work in us. But the people who knew us before we knew Jesus may not understand the change. Instead of being up front about who Jesus is and what He has done in our lives, we stay quiet. Why? We worry more about people’s opinions of us than their salvation. We worry they’ll think we’re strange, radical, or judgmental. But if the gospel is *really* the best news—if we’ve been saved from sin and death—how can we keep silent? How can we *not* share the best news with others?

## Discussion

<!-- segment: S0049 -->
**Main Point:** Following Jesus changes us and gives us new purpose.

<!-- segment: S0050 -->
### When We Find Jesus, We Tell Others

<!-- segment: S0051 -->
**Read John 1:35-46**

<!-- segment: S0052 -->
**WHAT DO PEOPLE SEARCH FOR IN LIFE? WHAT DID JESUS ASK THE TWO MEN (V. 38)? WHAT DOES JESUS’S RESPONSE SHOW ABOUT HIM (V. 39)?**

<!-- segment: S0053 -->
We’re all searching. We look for stability, peace, and joy; we seek belonging and acceptance. These longings aren’t bad. In fact, these God-given desires are designed to point us to *God*, who alone meets them fully. **Everything else comes up short because our souls are searching for a restored relationship with God.** Jesus asked, “What are you seeking?” implying, “What are you *searching for, hoping for, longing for*?” Then Jesus invited them to come and find it by following Him. **He invites us to come—to find what our souls are searching for in Him.**

<!-- segment: S0054 -->
**SHARE YOUR NICKNAMES. WHY DID JESUS RENAME SIMON?**

<!-- segment: S0055 -->
*Leaders: Share the meaning of Cephas or Peter or ask a student to look it up.* We don’t have control over our legal names. They’re chosen for us and can often help shape us. But nicknames usually come from something we’ve done or will do or from our character. Our nicknames can help shape our identity too. Jesus called Simon, Cephas or Peter—both meaning “rock.” Jesus wasn’t just giving Simon a nickname; He was transforming him, giving him a new identity and a new purpose. **God may not change your name, but He calls you His child (1 John 3:1), giving you a new identity and a new purpose.**

<!-- segment: S0056 -->
**HOW DID ANDREW AND PHILIP REACT TO REALIZING THAT JESUS WAS THE MESSIAH? WHY DON’T WE HAVE THIS SAME REACTION AND DESIRE TO *TELL OTHERS*?**

<!-- segment: S0057 -->
After spending a short time with Jesus and realizing He was the Messiah, Andrew ran to tell his brother, Simon, and brought him to Jesus. Philip did the same; he found Nathaniel and told him that Jesus was the One Scripture promised. Both men reacted the same to this realization: They ran immediately to tell others about who they had found—and how Jesus found *them*. The Jewish people had waited centuries for a Savior. The prophets pointed to Him. After waves of oppression and exile, Jews lived under Rome’s cruel power. They were expectant and hopeful for Israel’s rescue and restoration, but the rescue would extend much further. Jesus rescued humanity from sin, restoring us to God! When Andrew and Philip realized Jesus was the Messiah, they were overcome with the news. They *had* to tell others. Now, we know Jesus paid in blood for our rescue; we’ve experienced restoration in our relationship with God. **Yet, we often forget how *badly* we needed to be rescued—that we couldn’t do it ourselves. We lose awe and wonder for the gospel. We stop running to tell others what we’ve found—of who found us, Jesus. May we never get over our rescue or stop telling others how to be rescued too.**

<!-- segment: S0058 -->
### Jesus Sees Us and Knows Us

<!-- segment: S0059 -->
**Read John 1:47-51**

<!-- segment: S0060 -->
**HOW DID NATHANIEL FIRST REACT TO NEWS ABOUT JESUS? HOW CAN JESUS’S RESPONSE TO HIS QUESTIONS COMFORT US?**

<!-- segment: S0061 -->
Hebrew students learned through questioning. Questions prompt answers. Nathaniel’s first recorded words were questions (vv. 46,48). Let that sink in. Nathaniel heard about Jesus but had questions. Notice how Jesus described Nathaniel: a true Israelite who wasn’t deceitful! Jesus wasn’t bothered by Nathaniel’s questions. Jesus knew his heart—his desire to distinguish the real Messiah from false ones. Jesus was okay with Nathaniel asking questions because he desired truth. **God isn’t afraid of our honest questions. When we desire to learn and grow in our faith—when we seek truth with the pure intention to know God better—He’ll direct us to the right answers.** He’ll show us grace and patience, too, because the answers don’t come all at once—some never do on this side of eternity. But we get to walk with and learn from Jesus.

<!-- segment: S0062 -->
**HAVE YOU EVER FELT LIKE GOD DIDN’T UNDERSTAND YOUR LIFE OR SEE YOU? WHAT DOES VERSE 48 HELP YOU KNOW?**

<!-- segment: S0063 -->
You may struggle with feeling seen sometimes. The world is big—huge, actually. Our lives can feel so insignificant in a sea of other people and in a world with so much trouble. You’re not the only one who may wrestle with feeling like God doesn’t see you, think of you, or care about the details of your life. **God sees you; He sees all of us. And He doesn’t just see *where* we are but *who* we are on the inside. He sees our motives, our sin, our doubt. And He calls us anyway—from all of this—to follow Him and to let Him transform us from the inside out.**

<!-- segment: S0064 -->
**HOW DO PEOPLE TRY TO GET TO GOD OR HEAVEN ON THEIR OWN, APART FROM JESUS? READ GENESIS 28:10-17. WHAT DID JESUS REVEAL ABOUT HIMSELF IN JOHN 1:51?**

<!-- segment: S0065 -->
People have tried everything to reach heaven on their own, even going so far as building a huge tower (Gen. 11)! We try so hard to be good enough: We live morally; we help the poor; we go to church; we donate to charities. But our human efforts are simply towers, which will never reach high enough. Jesus referred to Jacob’s (Israel’s) vision of a ladder going to heaven and explained that *He* was the ladder. **Humanity’s best efforts would never be enough, but on Jesus’s very body and by His blood, we can ascend to heaven and be with the Father.**

## Wrap-Up

<!-- segment: S0067 -->
**IF PEOPLE “COME AND SEE” WHERE YOU’RE GOING IN LIFE (V. 46), WHERE WOULD YOU LEAD THEM? WHAT NEEDS TO CHANGE SO THAT YOUR LIFE LEADS TO JESUS?**

<!-- segment: S0068 -->
Our lives say, “Come and see,” even when we aren’t actively inviting people to do so. Many of us want others to see *us first*. We point people to our accomplishments, our intelligence, our talents. We desperately want them to see our physical beauty or our strength. We showcase our possessions and our wealth. Every bit of this fades. **We waste our days pointing to what doesn’t last.** Instead, the way we live each day should cry out, “Come and see Jesus!” Come and see the One who lives forever and shares His inheritance with us! Come and see the Son of God and what He did for us! Come and see how He transformed my life of sin into a life of holiness! Come and see freedom instead of addiction, security in place of insecurity. Come and see God’s love for you. How do we do this? **We actively walk with Jesus; we can’t lead others to Him if we aren’t following Him. And as we walk with Jesus—as we grow and learn and enjoy real relationship with Him—we become *like* Him.** We become His ambassadors, pointing to Him always.
