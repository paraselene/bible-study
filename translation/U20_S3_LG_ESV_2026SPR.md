## Context

<!-- segment: S0031 -->
The Greek word “devil” comes from the Hebrew word *sātan*. *Sātan* is a term for an accuser, slanderer, or adversary, rather than a proper name. We see many references to *sātans* in the Old Testament. But over time, the term became synonymous with Satan, described in the New Testament as “an enemy (Matt. 13:39); the evil one (Matt. 13:38); a tempter (Matt. 4:3; 1 Thess. 3:5); an adversary (1 Pet. 5:8); the father of lies (John 8:44); a murderer (John 8:44); a liar (John 8:44); a deceiver (Rev. 10:9); an accuser (Rev. 10:10); and one disguised as an angel of light (2 Cor. 11:14).”¹³ We know that Satan is limited; we can resist him and are urged to do so because he flees when we do (James 4:7). We often inflate the devil’s powers. God has given him limited power in the world—for now. His influence has earned him titles like, “god of this world” (2 Cor. 4:4) and “ruler of this world” (John 12:31). But never do we see that the devil can control or read believers’ minds, enter our bodies, or be everywhere at once. In no way is he like our Lord, Jesus Christ, who has dominion over all of heaven and earth, now and forever!

### Theology Connection

<!-- segment: S0033 -->
#### Temptation and Sin

<!-- segment: S0034 -->
Temptation is not the equivalent of sin. Instead, temptation can refer to natural and good desires that are twisted and directed toward pleasing self rather than giving glory to God. Jesus was tempted like we are (Matt. 4), yet He never sinned; He faithfully resisted temptation and followed the will of His Father. Knowing our weakness, we are to be on guard against temptation that may lead us to sin (Matt. 26:41), and we pray for God to deliver us from evil (Matt. 6:13).

### 2. Cultural Connection

<!-- segment: S0048 -->
The world teaches us that sin isn’t a big deal; it’s normal to experiment; we’re only young once. Culture lowers the bar for sin, or eliminates it completely, and teaches that we all have our own truth. We try our best to keep these ideas from permeating our hearts and minds, but they creep in—especially if we stop paying attention, if we crack open the door to a sinful desire instead of closing it and walking away. We think we can handle just a little bit of sin and stop anytime. Sin doesn’t work on those terms; sin is slavery that leads to death (Rom. 6:16). And when you give it room, sin seeks to invade every part of you. You may sometimes feel like you *have to* sin because you don’t have a choice, but this is another lie from the enemy of your soul. You have the power of the Holy Spirit living within you, directing you to the way out. God won’t allow you to be tempted more than you can handle (1 Cor. 10:13), but you have to use the tools God has given you to stand: His Word and the help of His Spirit.

<!-- segment: S0051 -->
### God’s Word Is Our Weapon

<!-- segment: S0052 -->
**Read Luke 4:1-4**

<!-- segment: S0053 -->
**HOW DID JESUS WIND UP IN THE WILDERNESS? WHAT DOES THIS SHOW YOU ABOUT OUR LIVES?**

<!-- segment: S0054 -->
After Jesus’s baptism, the Spirit, not Satan, led Him to the wilderness to be tempted. God led; Jesus obeyed. God allowed Satan to tempt Jesus to confirm His Sonship and to model how to overcome temptation. God allows us to be tempted, but He never does the tempting. Giving in to our sinful desires is what causes us to sin (James 1:13-15). God sometimes brings us to our own wilderness to face testing and trials to strengthen our faith (James 1:2-3). Jesus was no stranger to suffering, and we won’t be either, but we can trust that wherever the Spirit leads is for our good—to help us become more like Jesus, who endured temptation to give us grace and mercy in ours (Heb. 4:15).

<!-- segment: S0055 -->
**WHY DO YOU THINK SATAN TEMPTED JESUS WITH BREAD FIRST? WHY WOULD MANY PEOPLE JUSTIFY HIM GIVING IN?**

<!-- segment: S0056 -->
Jesus had fasted for forty days. Satan knew Jesus’s body demanded food. Physical hunger created an opportunity for Jesus to act apart from the Father instead of trusting His provision. We might wonder, “What was so wrong with Jesus providing food for Himself? Hadn’t He earned it?” The problem was that Satan began his temptation with “If you are the Son of God . . .” **Jesus had no reason to prove Himself to Satan; Satan knew Him well.** God had also confirmed Jesus’s identity at His baptism. **Unlike the Israelites who failed to trust God’s provision in the wilderness, Jesus passed the test.**

<!-- segment: S0057 -->
**WHAT *TEMPTATIONS* DO TEENAGERS FACE TODAY? READ DEUTERONOMY 8:3. HOW DOES SEEING JESUS APPLY THIS VERSE SHOW YOU THE WAY TO FIGHT TEMPTATION?**

<!-- segment: S0058 -->
Many of us struggle with comparison, focusing our attention on what others have. Temptation strikes at our identity, leading us to question how God made us. Temptation pressures us to lower the bar for sexual purity; we’re lured to harmful websites and into toxic relationships. We are tempted to escape real life through substance abuse. We wrestle with finding our worth in worldly approval and popularity. Deuteronomy 8:3 says that God’s Word is more critical than physical food. In other words, we can’t survive spiritually without it. Here’s the reality: **Jesus didn’t pull His “Son of God” card or use supernatural powers to defeat temptation; He battled Satan’s lies and temptations with the exact weapons we have: the Word and the Spirit!** Jesus is the Son of God. But He wasn’t sinless because He tapped into some power we don’t have available to us. Jesus was human in every way, yet He defeated temptation because He chose to obey the Father’s Word. He showed us how to stand—by knowing and living according to God’s Word and by following the Holy Spirit’s voice. **Though standing strong against temptation may seem way more difficult than this, it’s this simple: God’s Word sustains us. We can’t live without it.**

<!-- segment: S0059 -->
### Satan Flees When We Cling to Truth

<!-- segment: S0060 -->
**Read Luke 4:5-14**

<!-- segment: S0061 -->
**READ PSALM 119:11. HOW DID JESUS APPLY THIS VERSE? CAN WE REALLY DO THE SAME?**

<!-- segment: S0062 -->
Satan could never *make* Jesus sin; He could only suggest it. Didn’t the serpent convince Eve to sin by suggesting that she misunderstood God’s words: “Did God actually say . . .” (Gen. 3:1)? Isn’t this what Satan does with us too? **He can’t make us sin; he can only suggest it.** When we are tempted by our sinful desires and have a weak understanding of God’s Word, we allow the enemy’s suggestions to cause doubt and for doubt to become justification: “Well, maybe this isn’t *really* a sin? The Bible is outdated; culture has changed. Things have different meanings now. Surely, God didn’t really mean . . .” Adam and Eve allowed doubt to win, but Jesus showed us how to stand—on a firm foundation of the Word. **He refuted every lie and every suggestion with truth; every word from Jesus’s mouth was the Word. God’s truth is like a light that exposes the enemy’s lies and shuts them down!**

<!-- segment: S0063 -->
**IS SIN EVER UNAVOIDABLE? LOOK AT 1 CORINTHIANS 10:13. WHAT DOES THIS VERSE SHOW YOU?**

<!-- segment: S0064 -->
Satan tempted Jesus with “every temptation” (v. 13), and we can see three types in today’s text: needs, power, comfort. We often struggle in these same areas. We worry God won’t meet our needs, so we look some place else; we desire power and status, so we strive for popularity over holiness; we seek comfort over suffering and avoid the more difficult road of faith. We often put ourselves in situations where sin feels unavoidable, but we always have a choice to surrender our sinful desires and take the way out God promised to give us. **We sin because we want to sin—because it’s enticing. But as we walk with Jesus, He changes our hearts to desire Him instead!**

<!-- segment: S0065 -->
**LOOK AT VERSE 10. WHAT DID SATAN USE AGAINST JESUS? READ 2 TIMOTHY 4:2-4. WHAT HAPPENS IF WE DON’T KNOW AND RIGHTLY UNDERSTAND SCRIPTURE?**

<!-- segment: S0066 -->
The situation is comical. Satan tried to use God’s Word against THE Word (John 1:1)! Jesus knew the Word and was full of the Spirit. Satan had no chance of bending truth. **If we are going to stand against Satan’s strategy of bending truth to confuse us, then we can’t simply *know* the Word; we must have a right understanding of it—its context and how it applies.** If not, Satan will weaponize truth by blending it with lies, convincing us to trust teachings that sound good but lead us astray.

## Wrap-Up

<!-- segment: S0068 -->
**BASED ON THE PASSAGE, HOW MUCH POWER DOES SATAN HAVE IN TEMPTING YOU? HOW CAN YOU DEFEAT TEMPTATION THAT KEEPS DEFEATING YOU?**

<!-- segment: S0069 -->
We often credit Satan for our sin. We say things like, “The devil made me do it.” Let’s get rid of that mentality because Satan has no power over God’s children. He can’t read your mind; he can’t be everywhere at once; he can’t force you to act on sinful desires. But he knows human nature because he has watched human history. The truth is that we have the power to walk away when we’re tempted. **We can walk different paths and avoid the way of sinners (Ps. 1:1), but instead, we so often strive to walk alongside them—to get as close to sin as possible without touching it.** Then we wonder why we keep falling into sinful patterns we’ve desperately tried to break on our own. We must look at where we’re walking and where we’re planted (Ps. 1:3). **If we want to defeat temptation—if we’re tired of giving in and doing what we don’t want to do—then we must use the powerful weapons God gave us: His Word and His Spirit.** We must actively engage with the Word—deeply and daily—and walk by His Spirit (Gal. 5:16-17).
