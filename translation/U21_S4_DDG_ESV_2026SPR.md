### BRIDE OF CHRIST

<!-- segment: S0019 -->
The church is described as the bride of Christ, faithfully waiting for the day when Christ will return and heaven and earth will be one. Made up of all believers from all tongues and nations, the church is the bride that Christ redeemed. The picture of a bride also speaks to the permanence of Christ’s relationship with the church, for marriage was intended by God to be a lasting covenant between a man and a woman (Gen. 2:24; Matt. 19:5).

## CULTURAL CONNECTION

<!-- segment: S0028 -->
Why do jealousy and competition exist between believers and in the church? Real answer: People are sinful; people make up the church. Jealousy creeps in, even between friends who follow Jesus. Maybe a friend is making an impact on others, and you feel jealous because you know his faults. Jealousy urges you to point them out instead of celebrating how God is changing your friend for the better and using him to help others. Maybe you desire attention and applause. You see people receiving attention for their good decisions, and you struggle with bitterness. Feeling jealous or competitive reveals our motive: We want the spotlight. Jealousy and competition harm the church, causing division and bitterness and shifting our focus off of Jesus and onto ourselves. We all struggle with these feelings, but we must recognize them immediately instead of allowing the feelings to simmer. We can confess how we feel to God and to the other person and ask God to help us shift our focus onto Him. We can clap for others and celebrate their wins. The truth is that following Jesus isn’t about attention or applause. Following Jesus means pointing to Him and away from ourselves because He is the One worthy of glory.

# DAY ONE

<!-- segment: S0030 -->
**MATTHEW 20:20-28**

<!-- segment: S0031 -->
Have you ever watched small children form a line? If so, you probably saw several of them race and push to be first in line. Being the line leader is a high privilege when we’re young! The position makes us feel powerful over our peers. Our world encourages us to elevate ourselves—to do whatever it takes to be first, to be seen, to be applauded. Jesus taught another way.

<!-- segment: S0032 -->
In today’s passage, the mother of James and John, two of Jesus’s disciples, asked Jesus to promise that her sons would have seats next to His throne in the future kingdom. She wanted her sons to have the highest place of honor. Jesus used this moment to teach an important, radical truth: If we desire to be great, then we must serve and put others first. This concept was backwards and went against cultural norms. Jesus made the point that even He, the Son of God, came to serve and to give His life for others. He calls us to do the same—to lay down the desire for recognition in exchange for serving others, even when we receive no applause.

<!-- segment: S0033 -->
Think for just a moment about what happens when you receive attention from others. Sure, attention feels good and can encourage us to keep going, but more often, pride starts to sink in. We begin to make unwise decisions, hurting not only ourselves but others too. Now, think of a time when you served or helped others out of the desire for God to use you. Chances are not only did that feel better than becoming prideful but you likely felt greater peace and purpose. The Bible teaches us that Jesus should be elevated above all things. We can and should pursue great dreams and goals and strive for success. But we do these things to elevate Jesus and show Him to others. When we point to Jesus instead of to ourselves, we enjoy lasting satisfaction rather than fleeting applause from others.

# DAY TWO

<!-- segment: S0038 -->
**PHILIPPIANS 2:1-4**

<!-- segment: S0039 -->
What separates humans from every other creature is that we are made in God’s image. It’s not that animals, bugs, birds, fish and other things don’t matter; they just aren’t made in God’s image! We’re the only ones who get to be God’s managers for everything on earth. Only humans love others the way that God does. How? Because of Jesus living in us. If this is true (and it is), then that means that every single human in the world matters!

<!-- segment: S0040 -->
In our culture, we are often tempted to elevate ourselves over others. We may think that we have more to offer or are better than other people because of the country, the town, or the neighborhood we live in. This is a very dangerous mindset because none of these things matter when it comes to our eternity. The car we drive, the shoes and clothes we wear, the perfect test scores, and the state championship can’t make us right with God. Only a relationship with Jesus can do this. Jesus repeatedly called out religious leaders for viewing themselves as better than others. We must examine if we’re guilty of the same.

<!-- segment: S0041 -->
Paul taught what this idea looks like. If we are walking with Jesus, then loving and serving others should come naturally. Why? Because walking with Jesus—really learning from Jesus—transforms us to live like He lived. Jesus modeled humility by coming to the world as a human, living a life of service and love to others, and then dying on a cross for our sins. If He is our example and He lives in us, then we should strive to live humbly too.

# DAY THREE

<!-- segment: S0046 -->
**PROVERBS 27:2**

<!-- segment: S0047 -->
Have you ever been around people who brag constantly about the things they’re good at? Even when everyone recognizes their talents, they still feel the need to announce them. Maybe you’ve struggled with this too. The truth is that we all fight this part of our human nature. We are designed to recognize glory—God’s glory—but our common struggle is desiring glory for ourselves. You may be an all-star athlete or a straight-A student. Maybe you’re a great reader or the best baker in the world! The truth is that we’re all good at something because God placed within each of us certain abilities and gifts. How do we use these gifts and talents wisely and humbly instead of using them as billboards to announce our own goodness?

<!-- segment: S0048 -->
We can be confident in God’s gifts without being prideful. Pride celebrates our ability but godly confidence points to God’s ability. When we hoard applause for ourselves, we’re standing on our own ability, and the truth is that we will fail at some point. Our ability is limited. But when we humbly use the gifts God has given us, we let go of needing people’s approval. Instead, we stand on the firm foundation of God’s ability that will never fail us and on His approval. We are confident only because we’re not standing on our own goodness! When we do something really great, the glory should go to God’s name. Jesus lives in us, empowering and strengthening us for good works. Other people may notice our success, but we will have greater impact on others when we direct their gaze to Jesus. Instead of announcing our wins, we can use any applause or attention to invest in eternity, in spreading Jesus’s name. This week, walk in confidence because of God’s good work and His ability!

# DAY FOUR

<!-- segment: S0053 -->
**JAMES 3:13-18**

<!-- segment: S0054 -->
Ambition is the desire and determination to reach a goal. Is having ambition wrong? Well, no—and maybe. James, the brother of Jesus, explained that bitter envy, which is the jealous desire for what others have, and selfish ambition lead to all kinds of evil. The key word is selfish. So, we can determine that ambition and selfish ambition are rooted in different motives: One strives to lift up God and the other strives to lift up self. We’ve established that pride is rooted in sin and that we need to rid our hearts of it each day. Sin directs us to look only at ourselves—at our wants and our needs. In other words, sin reinforces selfishness. We wrestle with selfishness on a daily basis—in the big and small choices we make in our classes, at the lunch table, with our teammates, in our friendships, in our homes, with our siblings.

<!-- segment: S0055 -->
James wrote about the dangerous outcome of a selfish lifestyle. He explained that ambition rooted in selfishness leads to “disorder and every vile practice.” As crazy as that sounds, selfish ambition lures us into doing whatever it takes, even when it’s sinful, to look out for our own good. Selfish ambition leads us to comparing and competing against others. When we stop comparing our lives and successes to others, we fight feelings of bitter jealousy and selfish ambition. Why? Because we find fulfillment and peace in pleasing God, not in pleasing ourselves. This is the way forward: We center all of our ambition on lifting up Jesus’s name.

# DAY FIVE

<!-- segment: S0060 -->
**PSALM 96**

<!-- segment: S0061 -->
We’ve been studying how glorifying the Lord fulfills us more than glorifying ourselves. In case you need a refresher, to glorify means to elevate above other things. You may wonder how glorifying someone else could possibly feel better than glorifying yourself, but we were created for this! God designed us to glorify Him, so we are most satisfied when we do what God designed us to do. God made us to enjoy Him forever, but sin distorts our view and convinces us that chasing worldly comforts will give us peace. Worldly comforts will always disappoint us because we weren’t designed to be satisfied by them. Let’s think about technology for a minute. Technology can be a great tool, but being addicted to it brings us down and affects our personal well-being. We seek validation from social media, but instead, we’re often left feeling like we’re not enough and comparing ourselves to other people. We seek joy and escape in video games, but they all come to an end. Our feelings of depression and anxiety remain. The truth is that we won’t—we can’t—find satisfaction and peace in worldly pleasures.

<!-- segment: S0062 -->
The writer in Psalm 96 experienced God’s goodness, especially in comparison to what the world offers. The writer was overwhelmed by God’s glory and power in creation. The psalmist reminds us that everything except the Lord and His kingdom will fade away. If you’ve experienced God’s goodness and His glory, then the worldly comforts you used to depend on won’t satisfy you anymore. They’ll come up short. Instead, you can look to God’s glory and join with creation in worshiping Him and proclaiming His goodness!
